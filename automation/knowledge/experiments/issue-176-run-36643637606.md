## Interpretation

- The storm breaker converts a systematic capacity mismatch from a 45-minute budget burn into a ~12-minute fail-fast with an actionable diagnostic, but it cannot make an oversized workload fit. With every validated env/config switch wired and still pinned at the ceiling, the reusable repair is the diagnostic's own second remedy: a smaller workload. The budget caps the transcript/history growth the task controls while preserving the real coding loop (inspect, one small edit, focused test, report), staying faithful to #58's "one small concrete improvement" plus "focused relevant tests" scope.
- Hermetic probes (issue #78) already proved config-only switches do not move the startup peak (all arms inside the noise band; floor is the Bun runtime plus static module-graph parse), and four live storms confirm it. Any future #58-class storm at a base containing this budget must look at the binary (smaller fork tracks #79/#89 via the #86 pipeline) rather than at more flags or tighter caps.
- Scoping to smoke mode keeps the blast radius minimal: E2E issues own their success criteria and their payloads are untouched.

## Reusable knowledge

- Once the full qualified low-memory profile is present at a failed base, env/config flag wiring is an exhausted repair class for ceiling storms: shrink the smoke workload (`SMOKE_TASK_MEMORY_BUDGET` via `resolve_task_text`) or ship a smaller binary instead.
- `resolve_task_text` is the single chokepoint both live dispatch paths share (`render-job.sh` shell flow and `render_controller.py`), so task-envelope repairs there cover every dispatch shape; mode-gating keeps E2E payloads untouched.
- A correctly firing storm breaker does not exonerate the workload: when the breaker fires as designed at a base already carrying all prior repairs, the next repair changes what the workload asks the worker to do (reaffirmed for run 36637940250 after the #75/#159/#164/#170 repairs).
