## Interpretation

- The storm breaker converts a systematic capacity mismatch from a 45-minute budget burn into a ~13-minute fail-fast with an actionable diagnostic, but it cannot stop the redispatch loop: with production fully wired, the reusable repair is to hold proven full-profile storms at dispatch time until the named premise changes (smaller qualified binary via the qualification chain, or a larger worker), instead of re-proving the same mismatch one storm at a time.
- The hold is conditional, not permanent, and names its lifting condition in data (`CAPACITY_MISMATCH_HOLDS[58].successor`): removing the entry when the successor premise lands is the protocol-compliant material premise change that re-enables real measurement. Exact-artifact bodies are explicitly excluded so the retired-contract machinery keeps sole ownership of that path.
- A correctly firing storm breaker does not exonerate the dispatch policy: when the breaker fires identically at a base already carrying every prior repair, the next repair looks at what keeps redispatching the doomed payload, not at the breaker (the #161 lesson applied to the capacity signature).

## Reusable knowledge

- A full-profile storm abort is a dispatch problem, not a harness or profile problem: when the storm breaker fires identically at a base already carrying every prior repair, hold the issue at dispatch until the successor premise lands instead of re-proving the mismatch.
- The capacity hold is conditional and data-driven (`CAPACITY_MISMATCH_HOLDS`): lifting it removes the entry when a smaller qualified binary or larger worker lands; exact-artifact bodies are excluded so the superseded machinery keeps sole ownership of that path.
- Every hold needs three forms to survive the #161/#165/#171 lessons: a pure library guard, an executor-named verdict with zero-cost enforcement on the live path, and an envelope-consumable CLI with stable exit codes -- a controller mirror alone never reaches the live scheduler path, and a log-only verdict is not durable evidence.
