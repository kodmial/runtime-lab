# Repair triple-restart job loss by allowing three same-worker resubmissions

## Hypothesis / objective

Repair the concrete defect that failed Render execution for source issue #9 in run 36422228148 (execute=failure, cleanup=success, smoke mode): the original runner job plus both allowed same-worker resubmissions were lost to three consecutive proven worker restarts, so the attempt failed fast with `resubmissions used: 2/2` even though the worker was healthy throughout and ample poll budget remained.

## Preconditions / changed premise

Material premise changed since the issue #47 repair: run 36422228148 exercised the two-resubmission path and lost ALL THREE jobs on the same healthy service with direct instance-rotation proof (67de372e1e5f -> 5a3329272140 -> 81873f2a0598 -> 3133df580cc1; uptimes 29.2 -> 53.0 -> 60.8 -> 61.2), at ~4.4, ~8.4, and ~3.7 minutes after each submit. This falsifies "two resubmissions suffice" as a bound: the vendor contract permits restarts at any time, and three live runs (36410676408, 36417263684, 36422228148) now show the cluster signature growing from two to three consecutive losses. The fix targets the resubmission bound only; poll budget, one-service invariant, and mandatory cleanup are unchanged. Fully offline; no live Render credentials or service used. Source issue #9 was not closed or modified.

## Observations

- The failed run behaved exactly as designed by the previous repairs: correct fail-fast classification (3 consecutive 404s per loss), correct health qualification (healthy at every loss), correct restart proof (instance rotation on all three losses), correct two resubmissions, then fail-fast on the third consecutive loss. Classification, detection, and diagnostics are not the defect; the bound is.
- The three job lifetimes (~4.4 + ~8.4 + ~3.7 minutes ≈ 16.5 minutes, ~18 minutes wall-clock including deploy/health) sit well inside the unchanged 140x20s (46m40s) poll budget, so a third resubmission would have had ~28 minutes of budget remaining -- enough for a smoke attempt to plausibly succeed.
- `render-job.sh` needed no logic change: the resubmission branch, counters, baseline carry-forward, in-flight (fallback-aware) payload tracking, and diagnostics are all parameterized by the lifecycle constant.

## Interpretation

Fail-fast plus two retries converts a double-restart cluster into a recovery, but Free-worker restarts observably cluster in threes (three consecutive proven replacements in ~18 minutes). The reusable fix is a third bounded same-worker resubmission of the reproducible payload: triple-restart clusters become retries, a fourth consecutive loss still fails fast, and every existing invariant holds (one service per attempt, same-worker policy, unchanged 140-iteration budget, mandatory verified cleanup).

## Reusable knowledge

- On Free workers with anytime restarts and in-memory job stores, two retries are insufficient when restarts cluster in threes: allow three bounded same-worker resubmissions (four job attempts total) within the unchanged poll budget, and keep failing fast on the fourth consecutive loss.
- Parameterize retry branches by the lifecycle constant so a bound change needs no shell-logic edit and diagnostics stay consistent (`resubmissions used: X/N`).
- Shell/HTTP fakes for consecutive losses must rotate a fresh instance id per loss; reusing the last instance makes the final loss read as "same worker process lifetime" and weakens the regression.

## Unresolved questions / next experiment

- Whether three resubmissions suffice for even larger clusters (four or more consecutive restarts in one attempt); the resubmit counter plus instance-rotation evidence in the diagnostics will answer from live runs.
- The worker-side trigger for the three replacements (OOM/resource pressure vs host maintenance) remains unproven; the `/health` resource signals plus per-job identity from issue #41 are the discriminating instruments on the next live #9 attempt.
- Mirror loss handling into the persistent-controller path (`render_controller._poll_job_terminal`), which still has payload-only visibility; deliberately left out of this scoped repair as in prior runs.
